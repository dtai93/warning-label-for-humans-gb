## Best eight, tightened

1. From #1: “May buy vegetables for a better version of themselves” -> “May buy vegetables for their future self, then order pizza.” Cut the abstract “better version”; make the contradiction concrete; end on pizza.
2. From #13: “May ask what you think, then explain why you're wrong” -> “May ask what you think, then tell you why you're wrong.” Cut “explain”; the blunt “wrong” is the turn.
3. From #3: “May save recipes for a life they do not lead” -> “May save recipes for a life they never cook.” Cut “do not lead”; “cook” gives the image and lands the joke.
4. From #31: “May check the forecast, then dress for the weather they wanted” -> “May check the forecast, then dress for the one they wanted.” Cut the repeated “weather”; end on “wanted.”
5. From #17: “May say 'no worries' while privately cataloguing every worry” -> “May say 'no worries' while keeping score.” Cut the explanation; end on the concrete social tell.
6. From #19: “May open the fridge again in case the answer has changed” -> “May open the fridge again, hoping for new options.” Cut the setup; “options” carries the wishful turn.
7. From #30: “May remember the perfect reply three days after the conversation” -> “May rehearse the perfect reply for the wrong conversation.” Cut the time-and-scene explanation; end on “conversation.”
8. From #9: “May buy a notebook to organise the notebooks they already own” -> “May buy a notebook to organise a life they haven't started.” Cut the repeated “notebooks”; “started” lands the admission.

## Freshness and tournament

The eight were screened for the judge who has seen 2,000 entries. The fridge, forecast, “no worries,” perfect reply, and notebook ideas felt too familiar. Four advanced:

- A: WARNING: May buy vegetables for their future self, then order pizza. (68)
- B: WARNING: May ask what you think, then tell you why you're wrong. (64)
- C: WARNING: May save recipes for a life they never cook. (53)
- D: WARNING: May check the forecast, then dress for the one they wanted. (68)

All six pairs were read in both orders: A beat B, C, and D; C beat B and D; D beat B. All four beat the strongest broad cliché, “WARNING: Contents may shift under pressure,” in both orders. A wins most decisively: the ordinary act, self-delusion, and concrete “pizza” turn land in one read.
